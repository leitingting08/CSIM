# Attention (Spatial Attention & Neglect)

## 1. Posner's model: three attention networks

| Network                 | Definition                                                                    | Main structures                                                                                           | Neuromodulator                     |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **Alerting**            | Achieving and maintaining a state of **high sensitivity** to incoming stimuli | Locus coeruleus, right frontal and parietal cortex                                                        | **Noradrenaline** (norepinephrine) |
| **Orienting**           | **Selecting** information from sensory input                                  | Superior parietal lobe, temporo-parietal junction, frontal eye fields, superior colliculus (and pulvinar) | **Acetylcholine**                  |
| **Executive attention** | **Monitoring and resolving conflict** among thoughts, feelings and responses  | Anterior cingulate, lateral ventral prefrontal cortex, basal ganglia                                      | **Dopamine**                       |

_The structures and neuromodulators come from the table and figure on the slide (Posner & Rothbart's summary)._

- **"Where's Wally?"** is an orienting / visual search demo.

## 2. The orienting network: Posner's cueing task (Posner & Cohen)

### 2.1 Design

- Fixate the centre. A **cue** (an arrow, or a flash at one location) appears, then a **target** appears and you respond as fast as possible.

| Trial type      | Meaning                              | Frequency                           |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| **Valid cue**   | Target appears where the cue pointed | **75–80%**                          |
| **Neutral cue** | The cue points nowhere               | 20–25% (neutral + invalid together) |
| **Invalid cue** | Target appears on the **other** side |                                     |

### 2.2 Results

- Reaction times (approximate values read from the slide graph): **valid ≈ 320 ms < neutral ≈ 355 ms < invalid ≈ 380 ms**.
  - **Benefit** = neutral − valid (attention already at the right place)
  - **Cost** = invalid − neutral (attention must **disengage, move and re-engage**)
- Attention can move **without moving the eyes** (covert orienting).

### 2.3 Inhibition of return (IOR)

- After a location is attended:
  - for **~100–300 ms**, detection there is **faster and more accurate**
  - then, for **~500–3000 ms**, detection there becomes **slower and less accurate**
- The graph plots RT against **CTOA** (cue–target onset asynchrony):
  - at short CTOAs, **valid** trials are faster
  - at longer CTOAs (from roughly 200–300 ms), the pattern **reverses** and the cued location is slower
- **Function**: IOR promotes **exploring new, previously unattended objects** during visual search, by preventing attention from returning to places already checked.

## 3. Two visual streams

- **Dorsal ("where") stream**: occipital → **parietal**. Spatial processing: location, movement, spatial transformations, spatial relations.
- **Ventral ("what") stream**: occipital → **temporal**. Object processing: colour, texture, pictorial detail, shape, size.
- **Hemispatial neglect** follows damage to the **dorsal / parietal** side, typically the **right parietal lobe** (see the brain scan on the slide). Damage to the ventral stream gives problems like **visual agnosia** (the last video in the deck).

## 4. Hemispatial neglect

### 4.1 What it is

- After right-hemisphere damage (often right parietal), patients **ignore the left side** of space: they don't eat from the left half of the plate, don't shave the left side of the face, bump into things on the left.
- Their vision itself may be intact. The problem is **attending to** one side.

### 4.2 Perception demos

- **"Which bar appears darker?"** Two mirror-image grey gradients that are physically identical in overall brightness.
- **"Which scale is larger?"** Two mirror-image black wedges.
- _These are the greyscales and grating-scales tasks (my addition; the slide shows only the images and questions):_
  - **Healthy people** show a slight **leftward bias** (pseudoneglect): they pick the stimulus whose dark or large end is on the **left**.
  - **Neglect patients** show a strong **rightward bias**.

### 4.3 Classic clinical tests

| Test                  | What the patient does                                                                                                                                                                |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Line cancellation** | Crosses out lines only on the **right** side of the sheet                                                                                                                            |
| **Line bisection**    | Marks the "middle" **too far right**, because the left part of the line is not taken into account                                                                                    |
| **Word reading**      | Reads **COWBOY** as "**BOY**" (left half missed)                                                                                                                                     |
| **Drawing copy**      | Copies only the **right half**: a flower with petals on one side, a clock with all the numbers crowded on the right, a house missing its left part, a scene missing the left objects |

### 4.4 Neglect of mental images: Bisiach & Luzzatti (1978)

- Patients from Milan were asked to imagine **Piazza del Duomo** and describe it from memory.
  - Imagining themselves **facing the cathedral**, they described only the buildings on the (imagined) **right**.
  - Imagining themselves **at the opposite end** of the square, they described the buildings they had **omitted** before, and now left out the ones on the new left.
- So the information is **stored in memory**. What is neglected is the **left side of the mental representation** (representational neglect).
- The slides show **Plaça de Catalunya** (Barcelona) as a local version of the same task.

### 4.5 Implicit processing: "attention, not perception deficit"

**Extinction**

- A frog alone (left) → "I see a frog". A sun alone (right) → "I see the sun".
- **Both together** → "I see a sun"; the frog on the left is **not reported**.
- The left stimulus is perceived when alone, but it **loses the competition for attention** when there is a rival on the right.

**The burning house (Marshall & Halligan, 1988)**

- The patient was shown two house drawings, one with **flames on the left side**.
- She said both houses were **identical**, but when asked which she'd prefer to live in, she **chose the one without fire**.
- The "unseen" fire **influenced her choice**.

**Semantic classification with priming**

- Task: is the picture to the **right** of fixation a fruit or an animal?
- **200 ms before** it, a picture appeared on the **left** (for 200 ms). The **5 patients denied categorically** that there were two pictures.
- Three conditions: **highly congruent (HC**, e.g. same picture), **congruent (C**, same category), **non-congruent (NC**, other category).
- RTs: **HC ≈ 740 ms < C ≈ 750 ms < NC ≈ 850 ms**.
- The unseen left picture **primed** the response: it was processed **up to the level of meaning**, without awareness.
- _This is the classic Berti & Rizzolatti (1992) paradigm; the slide doesn't cite it._

**Conclusion**: neglect is a deficit of **attention / awareness**, not of perception. The neglected information is still processed, sometimes up to its meaning.

### 4.6 Rehabilitation (video)

- Training to scan toward the left, e.g. visual scanning training and anchoring cues on the left. _The video's content is not on the slides._
